## Course Overview

I participated in the development and taught this course at RWTH Aachen introducing students to the modern theory of machine learning.
The curriculum mainly follows [Bishop, 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/Bishop-Pattern-Recognition-and-Machine-Learning-2006.pdf).

## Topics Covered

### Probability theory
- Main definitions
- Discrete and continuous random variables
- Estimation
- Bayes' theorem

### Linear models for regression
- Least squares and maximum likelihood
- Regularized least squares and maximum a posteriori
- Bias-variance decomposition with the square loss
- Model selection: train/validation/test, cross-validation
- Bayesian linear regression
  
### Linear models for classification
- Generative vs. discriminative methods
- Logistic regression and multiclass logistic regression
- Gradient descent

### Neural networks
- Feedforward and convolutional networks
- Computational graphs and backpropagation

### Gaussian processes
- Definition and conditioning 
- Implementation
- Building kernel functions
- Semi-parametric models

## Teaching Philosophy

I believe in:
1. **Understanding theory by doing**: I co-designed 100+ pages of exercises with solutions so students could challenge their understanding at various levels of difficulty
2. **Understanding practice by doing**: Students implemented fundamentals from scratch, without the use of advanced libraries, to understand inner machineries
3. **Mathematical rigor**: Formally proving all major results
4. **High-level perspectives on proofs**: Proofs are understood not only at a technical, but reasonings are clearly motivated and articulated beyond their line-by-line unfolding.

## Student Projects

Students explored topics like:
- Implementing linear regression and K-means on a data set from robotics
- Implementing and training a feedforward neural network in pure NumPy
- Implementing and training a convolutional network for classification in PyTorch
- Fine tuning pre-trained models
